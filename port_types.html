Video Ports

Types of Ports

This section is a small showcase of each of the prominent video ports. If you don't know about the digital or analog signals, please reference the Signal Types page. This section might have mistakes, so please tell me in the survey if there are any.

RCA


No machine-readable author provided. Mobius assumed (based on copyright claims)., Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

RCA (Radio Corporation of America) cables are one of the oldest AV connection types. RCA Component cables have a maximum resolution of 480i or 576i. RCA cables are identified using their three multicolored jacks. Yellow represents video, and red and white represent left and right audio channels. It is not used in many devices these days.

VGA


Katsuki at English Wikipedia, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

VGA (Video Corporation of America) cables are analog video connectors, released by IBM in 1987. VGA has a maximum resolution of 1080p. It has a 15-pin connector for each color (RGB) and for horizontal and vertcial sync data. VGA is also identifiable by its blue casing. It is used in older monitors and devices, but some devices still ship with VGA.

DVI


Evan-Amos, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

DVI, short for Digital Video Interface is a digital video-only connector. It works by sending uncompressed video digitally, allowing better quality as there is no analog-to-video conversion. DVI's maximum resolution can be either 1920x1200@60Hz or 3840x2400@30Hz, depending on the cable type (single-link or dual-link). You can identify it by its white casing and flat connector pins. VGA outlasted DVI for being cheaper and more convenient, so DVI is not present in many devices these days.

HDMI


Evan-Amos, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

HDMI, short for High-Definition Multimedia Interface, is a digital video and audio connector that works like DVI, sending uncompressed video digitally. It has a maximum resolution of either 720p, 1080p or 4K, depending on the cable type. It is compatible with eARC, allowing better audio quality. You can identify it by the "pressed-in" corners on the connector. It is present in virtually every modern device with output these days.

DP


Belkin, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

DP, short for DisplayPort, is a digital video and audio connector. It works by sending data in packets, like USB. This allows the standard to be updated easily. The latest version of DisplayPort, 2.1b, supports resolutions up to 8K. You can identify it by the “chip” at the bottom right of the connector. It is present even more than HDMI in virtually every modern device with output.
